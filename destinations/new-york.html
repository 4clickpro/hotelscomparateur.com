Where to stay in New York

Midtown Manhattan is the classic first-timer base — Times Square, Fifth Avenue and the theatres are all walkable. Lower Manhattan suits downtown sights and the waterfront. The Upper West Side is quieter and residential, right by Central Park. Across the river, Williamsburg and Park Slope in Brooklyn give you more room for the money and a neighbourhood feel, with the subway into Manhattan in 20–30 minutes.

Tips for finding a good New York hotel deal

  • Compare the same dates everywhere. The same room can cost a different amount on different booking sites.
  • Travel in January or February. The weeks after the holidays are usually the cheapest time to stay.
  • Consider Brooklyn or Queens. A short subway ride can cut the nightly rate a lot.
  • Watch for resort and facility fees. Many New York hotels add a nightly fee on top of the room rate.
  • Book weekday nights when you can. Weekend rates in Manhattan run higher than weekday rates.

New York hotel questions

What is the best area to stay in New York?

Midtown Manhattan suits first-timers who want the sights close by. Lower Manhattan suits downtown and the Financial District, the Upper West Side suits a quieter residential feel near Central Park, and Brooklyn (Williamsburg, Park Slope) suits more space for the money.

When are New York hotels cheapest?

January and February are usually the cheapest months, after the holidays. Summer weekends and December (holiday season) are the most expensive. Weekday stays are often cheaper than weekends.

Is it better to stay in Manhattan or Brooklyn?

Manhattan keeps you closest to the main sights and the subway runs everywhere. Brooklyn often gives you a bigger room for the same price and a more local feel, with a 20–30 minute subway ride into Manhattan.
