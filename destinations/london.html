Where to stay in London

Covent Garden and Soho put theatres, restaurants and shopping on your doorstep — the classic first-timer base. Kensington is calmer and greener, next to the big museums. The City is business-central and quiet at weekends. Shoreditch is the creative, younger side of town, and South Bank keeps you by the river between the London Eye and Tower Bridge.

Tips for finding a good London hotel deal

  • Compare the same dates everywhere. The same room can cost a different amount on different booking sites.
  • Travel in January or February. The weeks after New Year are usually the cheapest time to stay.
  • Stay near a Tube station. Location matters more than the exact neighbourhood.
  • Check weekend rates. Business districts are often cheaper at weekends, and leisure areas cheaper midweek.
  • Watch for big events. Major concerts, matches and exhibitions can push prices up fast.

London hotel questions

What is the best area to stay in London?

Covent Garden and Soho suit first-timers who want theatres, restaurants and sights on foot. Kensington suits museums and quieter streets. The City suits business trips, and Shoreditch suits a younger, creative scene.

When are London hotels cheapest?

January and February are usually the cheapest months. Summer and December run higher, and big events can spike prices, so compare early around those dates.

Is the London Underground easy from most hotels?

Yes — most central hotels are within a few minutes of a Tube station, and the network covers the whole city. Staying near a station matters more than the exact neighbourhood.
