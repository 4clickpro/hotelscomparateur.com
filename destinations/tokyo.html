Where to stay in Tokyo

Shinjuku is the all-rounder — transport links everywhere, nightlife and restaurants. Shibuya is loud, young and fun, right by the famous crossing. Ginza is polished and upscale, with department stores and fine dining. Asakusa keeps old Tokyo alive around Senso-ji temple, and Ueno is the budget-friendly pick near parks and museums. Everywhere, stay near a JR or metro station and the whole city opens up.

Tips for finding a good Tokyo hotel deal

  • Compare the same dates everywhere. The same room can cost a different amount on different booking sites.
  • Avoid cherry blossom peak. Late March to early April is the most expensive time to stay.
  • Check the room size. Central Tokyo rooms run small — compare sizes, not just prices.
  • Stay near a station. A hotel five minutes from a JR station saves time every day.
  • Consider business hotels. Clean, compact and well located — often the best value in Tokyo.

Tokyo hotel questions

What is the best area to stay in Tokyo?

Shinjuku suits first-timers who want transport links and nightlife. Shibuya suits energy and shopping. Ginza suits upscale stays. Asakusa suits traditional streets and temples, and Ueno suits budget stays near parks and museums.

When is the cheapest time to visit Tokyo?

January and February (outside New Year) and the rainy season in June are usually the cheapest times. Cherry blossom season in late March to early April and autumn leaves in November are the most expensive.

Are Tokyo hotel rooms small?

Rooms in central Tokyo are often smaller than in the US or Europe, especially in budget hotels. If space matters, compare room sizes when you book, or look at hotels in quieter districts where rooms tend to be larger for the same price.
